# Grow rice and prepare ingredients

## Baseline
Candidate r2 is unapplied. This is a bounded Farmer's Delight 1.3.4 procedure, not a complete farming catalogue or proof of this pack's effective gameplay.

## Evidence status
Exact shipped recipes and English descriptions support the materials, cutting outputs and shallow-water planting below.[1][2][3][4][5] No world generation, effective tags, crop growth timings, controls or configuration were tested. References to a knife tag or item ability are recipe predicates, not proof that another mod's knife qualifies.

## Prerequisites
- A crafting interface, 1 flint, 3 sticks and 4 items accepted as `minecraft:planks`: 1 stick is for the knife; 2 are for the board.[2][3]
- One Farmer's Delight Rice grain to plant; for a cutting demonstration, one Rice Panicle or one Cabbage. These are distinct items, not interchangeable inputs.[4][5]
- A shallow-water planting spot. Keep at least one grain out of the cooking batch so that your next planting is not consumed; this reserve is a planning recommendation, not a growth-rate claim.

## Steps
1. Craft a Flint Knife with flint directly above a stick (`m / s` in one column). The shipped result is one knife.[3]
2. Craft a Cutting Board with two rows of `stick, plank, plank` (`/## / /##`). The shipped result is one board; place it in your food-preparation area.[2]
3. Start a rice plot by planting a grain in shallow water. The shipped advancement says “Plant grains of Rice in a shallow water puddle”; invalid placement reports “This seed must be planted in shallow water.” Check that placement succeeds before allocating the rest of your grains.[1] This packet does not prescribe unverified water depth, substrate, growth duration or harvest controls.
4. When you have a Rice Panicle, put **that item** on the board and use the board with a qualifying knife/tool in hand. The shipped description says to use the board with a tool to break down an item. For one panicle the cutting recipe specifies **1 Rice and 1 Straw**; collect both before repeating.[1][4]
5. For a second useful preparation task, place one Cabbage on the board and cut with a qualifying knife/tool. Expect **2 Cabbage Leaves**, not two whole cabbages.[5] The recipe accepts `knife_dig` item ability or `c:tools/knife`; it does not name a sword.[4][5]
6. Put planting grains aside, then take cooking stock to [cook a small batch](https://faizsaifulnizam.github.io/minecraft-pack-wiki/guides/homestead-cooking-pot/). Start there with rice; the soup route additionally needs the specific ingredient tags displayed in the recipe. This handoff does not assume a leaf's membership in an unresolved tag.

## Expected result
You have a placed rice crop if the documented planting predicate is satisfied, a board/knife preparation station, and exact panicle or cabbage cutting outputs.[1][2][3][4][5] Growth speed and harvesting yield remain outside this static claim.

## Common failures
- **“Maybe with a different tool…”**: compare the current cutting recipe with your tool. Try the Farmer's Delight knife rather than assuming a similarly named external knife qualifies; effective ability/tag membership is unverified.[1][3][4]
- **“This doesn't seem cuttable…”**: check the item identity. Rice Panicle is the demonstrated cutting input; Rice grain is the cooking/planting handoff. Remove the wrong input using the game's displayed interaction, whose binding is not traced here.[1][4]
- **Planting refused**: relocate the grain to a shallow-water spot, matching the shipped error. Do not infer that a dry ordinary farmland row can substitute.[1]
- **No wild plants nearby**: the shipped descriptions mention beach coasts for wild cabbage and ponds in wet lands such as swamps/jungles for wild rice. Those are documented search hints, not a guarantee for an existing pack world.[1]
- **Conditional tip — separate seed and meal stocks**: if you only have a small supply, reserve a grain before cutting/cooking. Benefit: you retain a next-planting input; trade-off: one less grain in your immediate meal batch. The planting and cooking input identities are shipped; the reserve quantity is an author recommendation.[1][4]

# Cook and serve a small meal batch

## Baseline
Candidate r2 is unapplied. Materials and recipe payloads are from Farmer's Delight 1.3.4. This page delivers a first cooked-rice batch and a conditional vegetable-soup extension, not the entire meal catalogue.

## Evidence status
Shipped recipes establish pot crafting, ingredient predicates and outputs; shipped UI text establishes “Needs heat from below”, “Heated” and “Served on: %s”.[1][2][3][4] Interface slot workflow supplements these with the author's latest/default-config wiki, **not release-pinned to 1.3.4**.[7] Neither local recipe shown explicitly names a serving container: use the actual result-slot/container display instead of pretending the JSON proves a bowl. Effective recipe loading, tags, container defaults and heat-state logic were not executed.

## Prerequisites
- One wooden shovel, two bricks, five inputs accepted as `c:ingots/iron`, and one accepted `c:buckets/water` item.[2]
- One `c:crops/rice` input for the first recipe. [Prepare rice from a panicle](https://faizsaifulnizam.github.io/minecraft-pack-wiki/guides/homestead-rice-and-cutting/), but check that the current recipe accepts it.[3]
- A suitable heat location: the shipped heat-source tag includes magma block, lava cauldron and the Farmer's Delight stove; its nested tray tag lists lava, campfires and fire.[5][6] This is membership evidence, not a promise that every state of those blocks heats a pot. Prefer an already-established safe cooking area; do not handle exposed lava for this tutorial.
- Serving containers indicated by the result slot. Bowls are a common example in supplemental author documentation, not a local explicit container field for these two recipes.[7]

## Steps
1. Craft the pot with rows `brick, wooden shovel, brick`; `iron, water bucket, iron`; `iron, iron, iron` (`bSb / iWi / iii`). Check that the displayed output is **1 Cooking Pot** before taking it.[2] The recipe does not establish the returned bucket behavior; check the crafting interface rather than budgeting a guaranteed remainder.
2. Place the pot above a suitable heat source and open it using your current game interaction. Check the heat indicator: the shipped labels distinguish **Heated** from **Needs heat from below**.[1] Supplemental documentation also describes bubbling above a suitable heat source.[7]
3. Put **one** accepted rice input in an ingredient slot; leave unrelated ingredients out for this first batch. The shipped cooked-rice recipe has one ingredient, result **1 Cooked Rice**, `cookingtime: 100` and experience `0.35`.[3] These are recipe values, not measured wall-clock timing or guaranteed experience receipt.
4. Wait for the meal/result indication, then read **Served on**. Following supplemental documented workflow, put the indicated container in its corresponding slot or interact with the pot while holding that container to take a serving.[1][7] Check that the serving enters your inventory and that the stored-meal indication changes. If the live GUI differs, do not move a whole ingredient stock into it.
5. If you want a vegetable-soup extension, check the current recipe for **one carrot-tag input, one potato-tag input, one beetroot-tag input and one leafy-green-tag input**: `c:crops/carrot`, `c:crops/potato`, `c:crops/beetroot`, `c:foods/leafy_green`. Its shipped output is **1 Vegetable Soup** and experience value `1.0`; this JSON specifies neither cooking duration nor container.[4] The local leafy-green tag points to `#c:foods/cabbage`; its effective nested membership is not established here.[8] Use [the accepted recipe-search guide](https://faizsaifulnizam.github.io/minecraft-pack-wiki/guides/recipe-search/) to identify currently accepted items instead of assuming every leaf works.
6. If you will carry that meal away from home, prepare [a Comforts sleeping bag](https://faizsaifulnizam.github.io/minecraft-pack-wiki/guides/homestead-comforts-rest/) for documented portable rest without setting a new spawn; the meal is travel stock, not a sleep prerequisite or interaction requirement.
7. Once a single serving works, repeat the same verified ingredient combination in a small batch. Keep planting stock separate and return to [rice preparation](https://faizsaifulnizam.github.io/minecraft-pack-wiki/guides/homestead-rice-and-cutting/) when grain supply is low. Store ingredients through [the accepted storage-choice guide](https://faizsaifulnizam.github.io/minecraft-pack-wiki/guides/storage-choice/) if useful; this link is organization guidance, not proof of pot automation compatibility.

## Expected result
The exact shipped first recipe produces Cooked Rice; the soup extension produces Vegetable Soup when its four predicates are met.[3][4] Serving is a documented workflow plus a live-interface checkpoint, not a claimed runtime test.[1][7]

## Common failures
- **Unheated**: check heat below the pot and the indicator, rather than blaming the rice. A listed tag member alone does not establish active state or any required placement gap.[1][5][6]
- **Ingredients sit without progress**: remove unrelated inputs; compare with the displayed current recipe and accepted tags. Do not substitute a Rice Panicle for a `c:crops/rice` input merely because both names contain “rice”.[3]
- **Meal exists but no takeable serving**: check the indicated serving container and corresponding slot/held item. Do not add the container as a seventh ingredient; documentation distinguishes six ingredient slots from the container slot.[1][7]
- **Soup tag ambiguity**: this packet deliberately does not certify cabbage-leaf eligibility. Follow the current recipe's accepted-input list; tag resolution is a remaining gap.[4][8]
- **Conditional tip — prove one recipe before scaling**: if tags or controls are unfamiliar, use one ingredient set and one serving container first. Benefit: distinguish heat, recipe and serving failures without committing your reserve. Trade-off: smaller immediate batch. This is an author technique based on the three separate documented checkpoints, not a throughput guarantee.[1][3][7]

4. With the faucet still unpowered, interact with the handle again to request stop. Put the collected expendable item back into the source; an empty source cannot prove shutdown. Keep the setup loaded and observe that the replenished sample remains in the source and no item appears under the nozzle for at least three documented one-second output intervals (at least three seconds). ### Apply bellows to an adjacent furnace
5. Craft bellows with rows `slab, slab, slab`; `leather, empty, leather`; `slab, slab, slab` (`000 / 1 1 / 000`). The shipped output is one bellows and its NeoForge condition requires the `bellows` flag.[2]
6. Place the bellows directly adjacent to the furnace with the air-facing front toward the intended target. The author documents facing any direction, blowing air when powered, and speeding furnace-like blocks right next to it.[4] No effect on the Farmer's Delight Cooking Pot is claimed; return to [the pot heat check](https://faizsaifulnizam.github.io/minecraft-pack-wiki/guides/homestead-cooking-pot/) for cooking rather than borrowing a furnace claim.

8. Remove power after checking. Keep entities away from the front: the same wiki documents pushing entities up to five blocks away and sustaining fire in front within two blocks.[4] Do not point it at exposed fire or an occupied walkway for the homestead task.

## Common failures
- **Recipe absent**: both recipes have feature-flag conditions. Check [current recipe availability](https://faizsaifulnizam.github.io/minecraft-pack-wiki/guides/recipe-search/); do not edit configuration or assume a shipped file necessarily loads.[1][2]
- **Faucet on/off seems reversed**: disconnect power for the initial handle check. The documented power behavior is inversion, so power and handle state need to be considered together.[4]
- **Item fails to appear**: recheck the inventory-below/item-output-under-nozzle arrangement and your single sample. A fluid source behind is a different documented task; no universal inventory support is promised.[4]
- **Bellows sounds but furnace does not accelerate**: recheck direct adjacency and air-facing orientation, then compare with a basic furnace before blaming fuel or spending more materials. “Furnace-like” is documentation, not a list of all compatible mod machines.[4]
- **Conditional tip — isolate power during commissioning**: if a shared redstone line is hard to reason about, use a separate controllable supply for the first bellows test and no faucet power. Benefit: distinguish placement/compatibility from faucet inversion; trade-off: temporary extra setup. The inversion and powered blowing are documented; this isolation is an author recommendation.[4]
- **Optional fluid extension, not delivered as a full task**: the wiki describes fluid sources behind, destination containers below, and 250 mB increments.[4] Accepted fluids, exact destination capacity, voiding behavior and pack-specific integrations need more evidence. Do not route valuable fluids based on this item-only starter.
